# 72 — The design seen: images, meshes and 3D, maps in the application

Status: To do
Depends on: 71
SPEC: [CLAUDE_PILOT_SPEC.md](../docs/CLAUDE_PILOT_SPEC.md) § 5.1, 6.1, 6.2, 6.3, 9, 10.1, 10.2; [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md) § 12

## Goal

Extend the physical view of the design of plan 71 in three directions:

1. **Images**: Claude sees the design as pictures (PNG), finer than 40 × 40 characters: the shape of the members, their curvature, the stress gradients along them, the deformed shape.
2. **Unstructured meshes and 3D**: the physical view works on any design laid on a mesh — triangles and quadrangles in 2D, tetrahedra and hexahedra, or 3D grids — not only on 2D grids.
3. **The maps in the application**: the user sees what Claude sees, in the Copilot view of a run: the maps of the design and its indicators, at the points Claude looked at, and the designs before and after each restart.

## Principle

- **One description, three renderings.** The `PhysicalDesign` protocol of plan 71 is generalized from a grid to a mesh: nodes, elements and their connectivity, or a grid as a special case (2D or 3D). The copilot derives from it, for any field: text maps (plan 71), PNG images for Claude, and the data of the maps of the application. The indicators (load path, members, material use, hot spots, features) work on the adjacency of the elements, whatever the mesh.
- **3D is read by slices and projections**, as an engineer reads a CT scan: sections along each axis at chosen levels, and projections (the maximum or the mean through the depth: where the material is, where the stress peaks). A 3D image adds an isometric view of the solid (the elements above a density of 0.5), when it is small enough to render.
- **Images complete the text, never replace it.** A text map and the indicators always come first (cheap, and readable by both backends in every case); images go through a tool Claude calls when the text is not enough, and count in the call budget (their tokens are estimated before sending, per image size).

## Scope

In, in `gemseo-claude-pilot`:

1. **Meshes** (`design.py`):
   - the protocol accepts a mesh: the coordinates of the nodes (2D or 3D), the elements (their nodes, their type), the elements holding each component of the design variables and of the constraints; a 2D or 3D grid stays the short form (its shape and cell size);
   - features, supports and loads located by coordinates or by elements;
   - the checks of plan 71 on a mesh (elements referring to existing nodes, fields of the right size);
   - the adjacency of the elements (sharing a face, an edge in 2D), computed once.
2. **Indicators on any mesh**: the load path, the connected parts, the hot spots and the changes on the adjacency graph; the width of the members from a distance transform of the solid on a raster of the mesh; the material use weighted by the element volumes.
3. **Text maps of a mesh and of 3D**: the mesh rasterized on a grid of at most 40 × 40 (each cell takes the elements whose centers fall in it, reduced as the field says); in 3D, the text map of a slice or of a projection, chosen by the tool (`get_design_view(field, point, view)` with `view` a slice `x=…`, `y=…`, `z=…` or a projection along an axis). The context of `stuck` gives the three projections of the density and of the stress ratio in 3D.
4. **Images** (`images.py`), rendered with Matplotlib (already installed with GEMSEO: no new dependency), Agg backend, in the copilot process:
   - 2D: the field on the mesh (or the grid) at full resolution up to a size (1,024 pixels on the largest side), the domain outlined, the supports and loads drawn, the features labeled, a color bar with the unit; optionally the deformed shape (displacements amplified) or the principal stress directions;
   - 3D: a slice or a projection as in 2D; the isometric view of the solid;
   - the tool `get_design_image(field, point, view, deformed?)`: the image returned in the result of the tool as an image block (the Messages API accepts images in tool results; the MCP tools of the Claude Code backend return an image content); the backends pass it through (`backends/api.py`, `backends/claude_code.py`), the fake backend records it;
   - the tokens of an image estimated from its size before sending (width × height / 750), refused when the call budget does not allow it; at most 4 images per exchange;
   - the prompt (`system_v4.md`): when to ask for an image, what to look for in it.
5. **Journal and report**: the images Claude saw stored next to the journal (`copilot_images/`, referenced by the `design` records, not inlined); the post-run report embeds the final design and the designs before and after each restart as images.

In, in `gemseo-lso`:

- the topology model offers its displacements for the deformed shape and the directions of its principal stresses;
- a 3D topology case, small (a cantilever of hexahedra on a 3D grid), to test the 3D path end to end; and one 2D case on an unstructured mesh of triangles (a bracket meshed with a hole), built without a mesher (a structured grid split into triangles, elements removed inside the hole).

In the application:

- the runner forwards a new event `copilot.design` (SPEC § 10.2): for each point Claude looked at, the reduced fields (at most 20,000 cells per map: a mesh or grid larger than that is reduced as for the text maps), the outline of the domain, the supports, the loads, the features and the indicators; the events of the journal replay them in the Results of a finished run;
- **the Copilot view** shows a **Design** panel: the map of a field chosen in a list (density, stress ratio, strain energy, price of the constraints, stationarity), drawn in **SVG with d3** (no canvas: rectangles for a grid, paths for a mesh), a color scale with its unit, the supports, loads and features drawn, a tooltip with the value of the cell; in 3D, the axis and the level of the slice chosen with a slider, or a projection;
- the point shown chosen among those Claude looked at (on the timeline of the decisions: clicking a decision shows the design it was taken on); a restart shows **before and after side by side**;
- the indicators under the map (load path closed or cut, gray and dead shares, hot spots) in words;
- the logic (reduction, color scales, outline, the list of the points) in `static/js/lib/design.js`, pure and tested with `node --test`.

Out:

- a 3D view the user can rotate (WebGL is not SVG): slices and projections only;
- meshes of more than 10⁶ elements in the images (reduced first);
- rendering the user's own post-processing (Paraview files, VTK export);
- the data levels (§ 7.1): the design, its images included, is sent at every level, as in plan 71.

## Tests

- A mesh: elements referring to missing nodes refused; the adjacency of a small triangulation; the load path of the bracket with a hole, closed then cut.
- The rasterized text map of the unstructured bracket; a slice and a projection of the 3D cantilever, character by character on a small case.
- The member width on a raster: a bar of known width.
- Images: the PNG of a small field has the expected size, its color bar and markers (checked on the figure objects, not on pixels); the token estimate of an image; an image refused over the call budget; a fifth image in an exchange refused. Each rendering under one second (small cases).
- Fake backend: `get_design_image` returns an image block recorded by the fake backend; the API backend builds a tool result with an image block (request checked, no call made); the Claude Code backend returns an MCP image content.
- Runner: `copilot.design` events, reduced to at most 20,000 cells; replay from a journal.
- `lib/design.js` (node): the reduction of a grid and of a mesh, the color scale, the slice of a 3D grid, the list of the points of the decisions.
- Every test under one second.

## Acceptance criteria

- By hand, with Claude Code: a piloted run of the bracket where Claude asks for an image when the text map is not enough, and describes what it sees in it (a member, a hot spot, the deformed shape); the same run with the unstructured bracket; a 3D cantilever where Claude reads the slices and projections.
- In the application: the Design panel of that run shows the maps Claude looked at, and a restart before and after.
- `python tools/check.py` passes.
