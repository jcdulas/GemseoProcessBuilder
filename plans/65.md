# 65 — Large-scale optimizer: core MMA and GCMMA

Status: Done
Depends on: 64
SPEC: [LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md](../docs/LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md) § 1, 3.1, 3.2 (without screening), 3.6–3.8, 4, 5, 11

## Goal

The core of `gemseo-lso` in `plugins/gemseo_lso/`: MMA and GCMMA written from Svanberg's papers, in NumPy and SciPy, running as a stepper whose state is saved and restored exactly. In this plan every constraint is in the working set (no screening yet): the core must first find the right optima on small problems, with a subproblem solver fast enough for 10⁵ variables and 10⁴ constraints.

## Scope

In:
- the package skeleton (MIT licence, `pyproject.toml`, README), declared in the repository's tool configuration (ruff, mypy, pytest paths) like `gemseo_claude_pilot`;
- `core/problem.py`: the `LargeScaleProblem` protocol (§ 5), and a `DenseProblem` built from Python callables for the tests;
- `core/approximation.py`: the MMA approximations (p, q, r computed from the rows, never stored for all constraints), the asymptotes and their update, the move limits, the elastic variables;
- `core/dual.py`: the dual of the subproblem — closed-form x(λ) clipped to the move limits, the dual value and gradient (two products with the rows), maximized with SciPy's L-BFGS-B, warm-started; and the primal-dual interior-point subsolver for small working sets (≤ `dense_dual_threshold`);
- `core/mma.py`: the outer loop of MMA; GCMMA's inner loop (conservativeness check, growth of ρ);
- `core/state.py`: the state (iterates, asymptotes, multipliers, counters), saved to and loaded from HDF5 with `h5py`; `core/report.py`: the report of an iteration (§ 5);
- the stepper: iteration by iteration, settings replaced between two steps, `stop(reason)`;
- stopping criteria (§ 3.8) and the settings of the core as a Pydantic model (§ 4, without the screening and row settings).

Out:
- screening, row cache, equality constraints (plan 66);
- GEMSEO (plan 68).

## Tests

- Svanberg's two-variable example and cantilever beam (1987): the published optima.
- Hock–Schittkowski problems with inequality constraints (HS 21, 35, 43, 76): their known optima.
- Constrained Rosenbrock: the same optimum as `NLOPT_MMA` and `SLSQP` within tolerance.
- Dual solver: for a random convex subproblem, the KKT conditions of the solution; L-BFGS-B and interior point agree.
- Stepper: a run saved at iteration 5 and restored ends at the same iterate, bit for bit, as the uninterrupted run; settings changed between two steps apply at the next one; `stop` ends cleanly.
- Every test under one second.

- The measured optimizer time per iteration meets § 3.7, or the notes say what limits it.

## Implementation notes

- **API**: the stepper is `Optimizer(problem, settings, state)` (the spec said `MMA(...)`); the method is a setting (`mma` or `gcmma`). The core settings are a frozen dataclass (`gemseo_lso.core.Settings`, NumPy and SciPy only), replaced between two steps with `dataclasses.replace`; the Pydantic models deriving from GEMSEO's come with the plugin (plan 68). The protocol gives `x0` (the spec had `size`). The array aliases live in `core/arrays.py` (a module named `types.py` shadows the standard library when a script runs from its folder).
- **Subproblem**: with Svanberg's standard form restricted to `a_i = 0` (no `z` variable), elastic variables `y_i` of cost `c y + d y²/2` (`elastic_cost` 1000, `elastic_quadratic` 1). The dual is maximized by SciPy's L-BFGS-B (`gtol` 1e-10), warm-started from the last multipliers. The interior point is written from the perturbed KKT conditions of the subproblem (not from Svanberg's `subsolv` code), reduced to a dense system in λ; its cost grows with m² n, so `dense_dual_threshold` is 100, not the 2000 of the draft spec. Both solvers agree to 1e-6 on random subproblems (tests).
- **MMA constants**: `ρ` = 1e-5 (MMA); GCMMA starts each outer iteration at `max(1e-6, 0.1 Σ|∂φ| range / n)` and raises `ρ` by `min(1.1 (ρ + δ/w), 10 ρ)` while an approximation is below its function at the candidate (tolerance 1e-9 relative). Asymptotes and move limits as in the spec.
- **KKT criterion**: a variable within 1e-8 of its range from a bound counts as at the bound (the interior point stops just inside). The residual uses the gradients at x_k with the multipliers of the subproblem that produced x_k.
- **Results on the test problems**: MMA and GCMMA find the optima of Svanberg's cantilever and two-ball examples and of HS 35, 43, 76, as SLSQP and NLopt's MMA do, in 8 to 34 iterations. HS 21 (x2 in [-50, 50] with a strong curvature) makes MMA oscillate for ever — its approximation is too flat and the asymptotes cannot come closer than 1 % of the range — while GCMMA converges: a known weakness of MMA, the reason for GCMMA. On Rosenbrock in a disk, MMA does not converge in 500 iterations; GCMMA reaches the optimum to 1e-7 but keeps oscillating by about 1e-6, the KKT residual stalling near 1e-4: `xtol_rel` stops it. The problems of GEMSEO are normalized to [0, 1] by default, which avoids the scaling of HS 21.
- **Measures** (12 cores, OpenBLAS, n = 10⁵, dense random rows): one dual evaluation 0.16 s at 2,000 constraints; a realistic subproblem (most constraints satisfied, 5 % near activity) solved in 3.5 s at 2,000 constraints and 8.2 s at 5,000 (about 20 dual evaluations): about 20 s expected at 10⁴ constraints, and ten times more at 10⁶ variables — within the targets of § 3.7. A nearly infeasible subproblem (hundreds of violated constraints) takes about 1,400 evaluations (4 minutes at 2,000 constraints): the dual is then piecewise (multipliers at the elastic cost, variables at their move limits); a diagonal scaling of the dual did not help. Left to the benchmarks (plan 69, open point 6). The runs at 10⁴ constraints and 10⁶ variables need more than the 32 GB of this machine (rows and their absolute values: 16 GB at 10⁴ × 10⁵).
- Memory: the rows and their absolute values are kept (2 × 8 bytes per entry), documented in the spec and the README.
- **Comparisons**: Rosenbrock in a disk is compared with SLSQP, and NLopt's MMA with HS 43 (NLopt's MMA does not settle on Rosenbrock within a test's second).
