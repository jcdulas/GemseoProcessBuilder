## Goal

In row mode, the rows of a constraint whose inputs come from an intermediate discipline are chained by vector-Jacobian products, and the full Jacobian of that intermediate is never formed. In the inner sizing of a structure, the reserve factors are asked for row by row through the internal loads (`nastran_sv`'s `DesignProblem.vjp`, one adjoint per row and subcase), instead of the Jacobian of the loads, which costs one solve per design variable. Milestone M3 of the sizing loop (ECOSYSTEM-EVOLUTIONS.md § 6).

## Implementation notes

- Measured first, on today's path (two constraints `Stresses` and `Buckling` fed by one `PlainLoads`, one batch): the intermediate was linearized once per point — GEMSEO's cache of the discipline served the second chain — but its whole Jacobian was formed, and multiplied by each chain.
- Open point 8 confirmed: the weights of several outputs per call; the weights of all the constraints fed by one intermediate are stacked on the union of their rows.
- Built as `Chaining` in `gemseo_lso.gemseo.sources`, created by the adapter from the sources of the constraints: the union of the upstream chains sorted once (producers first), the inputs of each intermediate leading to the design variables (its `input_names`) and its outputs read downstream. `Source.upstream` is now that list (no `MDOChain`); `rows_of`, `directional_of` and `Source.upstream_jacobian` are replaced by `Chaining.rows` and `Chaining.directions`. The upstream disciplines are executed once per point (GEMSEO's caches serve them); a discipline without the products is linearized once per point (a copy of its Jacobian kept for the point).
- The protocols are detected by their methods being present and callable (a subclass may set one to `None`).
- `row_batch_size` splits the weights of an intermediate; since the core already splits its requests at that size, one call per request in practice (`test_the_products_by_batches_of_rows` sets the adapter's setting alone).
- Rows are dense as soon as one product is dense (the adjoint products of a solver are); the sparse blocks are added in place (`np.add.at`) rather than through a second dense copy.
- The gradient of the objective is still GEMSEO's: an objective computed from the outputs of an intermediate offering the products makes GEMSEO linearize it. The tests and the benchmark keep the objective in its own discipline (`Mass`, `VolumeDiscipline`); the README says so. Chaining the objective's gradient through the products as well (one more row of weights) is left for a later plan.
- Benchmark (`benchmarks/run_split_topology.py`, `benchmarks/topology/split.py`; `StressTopology.stress_terms` factored out of `state`, same arithmetic): optimizer iterations at 10⁵ elements from full density are dominated by the dual of the subproblem (about 30 s per subproblem with 1,000 dense rows, and repairs), the same on both paths, so the ladder measures what the model costs per iteration — the evaluation and the rows of 1,000 constraints — through `GemseoProblem`, each run in its own process, the fastest of three. Results in LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md § 9: at 10⁴ elements 1.0 s / 0.57 GB against 11.6 s / 4.9 GB; at 10⁵, 22.7 s / 5.8 GB measured against 1,630 s / 472 GB extrapolated for the full Jacobian (lower 95 % bounds 958 s, 453 GB; ladders R² ≥ 0.998). Every run under a minute (the longest, products at 10⁵: 33 s).
- Acceptance "the same optimum": the rows of both paths agree to 6·10⁻¹⁴ at the iterates of a run, and the synthetic chain ends at the optimum of the full-Jacobian path (`test_a_whole_run_never_forms_the_jacobian_of_the_loads`). On the topology problem, the optima of the two paths differ (volumes 0.3657 and 0.3654 on 784 elements, both feasible) by as much as perturbing the rows of the products by 10⁻¹² moves them (0.3672): the MMA subproblems amplify the last digits into other local optima. Two iterations at 10⁴ elements already differ in the fifth digit of the volume. Accepted by the owner on 2026-10-23 as recorded in the acceptance criteria: the rows agree to 6.3·10⁻¹⁴ (≤ 1e-12, `test_the_rows_equal_those_of_the_full_jacobian_path` and the benchmark's `agreement`), the synthetic chain ends at the same optimum, and on 784 elements both optima are feasible with a volume gap of 0.084 % (< 0.5 %); `gemseo_lso.benchmarks.topology.split.acceptance` judges it, the benchmark's `acceptance` step records the verdict (accepted), `test_the_same_optimum_as_the_owner_accepted_it` checks the rule. Closed.
