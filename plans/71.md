# 71 — Claude understands the design: physical maps and restarts

Status: Done, but for the acceptance by hand (a run piloted with Claude Code, left to the user)
Depends on: 70
SPEC: [CLAUDE_PILOT_SPEC.md](../docs/CLAUDE_PILOT_SPEC.md) § 4.2–4.5, 4.7, 6.1, 6.3, 9; [LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md](../docs/LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md) § 5, 8

## Goal

When a run does not converge, Claude understands the design it is looking at as an engineer would: what each quantity means physically and in which units, where the structure is held and loaded, how the load travels through it, where the material works and where it is dead weight, where the stress concentrates and why. From this it explains what blocks the run in physical terms (a stress singularity at a reentrant corner, a load path that does not reach the supports, gray material the penalization makes inefficient, members thinner than the filter can resolve) and proposes a targeted restart from another design, instead of a mere change of settings.

Why: in the first piloted run of the bracket (plan 70, notes), Claude received quantiles of 10⁴ numbers without meaning attached: it concluded that the design was "gray everywhere" while 67 % of the densities were at a bound, and its decisions (a smaller `move_limit`, a larger `keep_factor`) were reasonable but blind to the structure. The runs of the bracket also end in different local optima (volumes 0.4389 to 0.4408 from perturbations of 10⁻¹⁵, plan 70 notes): a restart from a physically better design is the lever, not a setting.

## Principle

Three layers, from the model to Claude:

1. **The model describes its physics** — it is the only one to know it. A discipline with a design on a grid declares, through a protocol:
   - the **physical problem** in a few sentences: the physics (2D linear elasticity in plane stress), the material law and its interpolation (SIMP, penalty 3), what the objective and the constraints are (the volume fraction; the von Mises stress relaxed by the density, relative to its limit), the filter and what it implies (a minimum member size of about twice its radius), the units;
   - the **domain**: the shape of the grid, the size of a cell, the cells inside the domain, and its **geometric features** (a reentrant corner, a hole, a free edge), named and located;
   - the **boundary conditions**: the cells of the supports (and what they block) and of the loads (direction, magnitude);
   - the **fields**, each with its physical meaning: the quantity, its unit, its range and how to read it (thresholds, what high and low mean), how to reduce it over a block (mean, maximum), and where it comes from — a design variable, a constraint, or computed by the model at a point: the physical (filtered) density, the von Mises stress, the strain energy density (where the load travels), the sign of the major principal stress (tension or compression), the displacement.
2. **The copilot interprets** — generically, from the description, with no model-specific code:
   - **maps as text** (at most 40 × 40 characters, larger grids reduced by blocks as each field says), the supports and loads marked on them, each with its legend, unit and reduction factor;
   - **physical indicators**:
     - the **load path**: whether solid material (physical density above 0.5) connects every load to the supports, and where it is cut;
     - the **members**: their widths against the minimum member size of the filter (a member below it cannot be resolved and stays gray);
     - the **material use**: the share of gray material (inefficient under the penalization: it costs volume for little stiffness), of dead material (solid but with a strain energy below a small share of the mean: removable), of the volume in each member;
     - the **stress hot spots**: the connected zones of violated or active stress, their peak, their size, and the geometric feature they sit at (a hot spot at a reentrant corner is a singularity of elasticity: refining or tightening does not remove it, rounding the corner does);
     - the **price of the constraints**: the multipliers of the optimizer laid on the grid — how much volume each stress limit costs — and the stationarity per variable (where the KKT residual comes from);
     - the **changes** since the last call: material added and removed, hot spots moved.
3. **Claude reads them with a physics primer** in its prompt: SIMP and why gray material is penalized, the relaxation of the stress constraints and the singular optima of void elements, stress singularities at reentrant corners, load paths and their redundancy, the minimum member size, checkerboards; the restarts each diagnosis calls for.

The topology benchmark (plan 69) is the first model with a physical description.

## Scope

In, in `gemseo-claude-pilot`:

1. **Physical description** (`design.py`): the protocol `PhysicalDesign` a discipline may implement (structural, like `RowJacobian`: no import of the copilot in the model): `physical_description()` gives the problem, the domain, its features, the boundary conditions and the fields with their meaning; `physical_fields(x)` gives the fields computed by the model at a point. The pilot finds it on the disciplines of the scenario, like the row mode finds `RowJacobian` (LSO spec § 6), and checks it (cells inside the grid, fields of the right size).
2. **Interpretation** (`design.py`): the maps, their reduction and legends; the indicators above (load path, members, material use, hot spots and their features, price of the constraints, changes).
3. **In the context** (`context.py`, `detectors.py`, `tools.py`):
   - the physical problem in the context of every call, once (it does not change): a few hundred tokens;
   - a detector `stuck` (§ 4.2) from the reports of the optimizer and the history: the best KKT residual has not decreased by 10 % over 20 outer iterations while above 10 × `kkt_tolerance`, or the run stays infeasible over 20 iterations, or MMA switched to GCMMA and still oscillates;
   - when `stuck` fires, at the pre-run review and at the post-run report: the indicators, and the maps of the physical density and of the stress ratio at the best point, within the budget of 8,000 tokens (measured; the other maps through the tool);
   - the tools `get_design_view(field, point)` (a map at the best point, the current point or an evaluation, with its legend), `get_design_indicators(point)` and `list_design_transforms()`.
4. **The restart** (`decisions.py`, `guardrails.py`, `pilot.py`):
   - a new kind of decision `restart`: a starting design given by a base (the best point, the current point, a past evaluation) and a sequence of transformations on the grid: `binarize(threshold)`, `smooth(radius)`, `set_region(shape, value)` with a rectangle or a disk in the coordinates of the maps (to fill a zone, empty it, round a corner), `connect(from, to, width)` (a solid bar between two cells, to restore a load path), `blend(evaluation, weight)`; a restart names the physical diagnosis it answers;
   - applied as a **new segment** (§ 4.4) whose starting point is the transformed design; the database and the budget are kept; for `LSO_*` a fresh optimizer state (asymptotes, multipliers and working set start again);
   - guardrails: the transformations exist and their parameters are valid, the regions inside the grid, the design clipped into the bounds, at most `max_restarts` restarts per run (default 3), never with less than 20 % of the budget left; a refused restart is sent back to Claude once, as for other decisions;
   - at the next call, the context compares the indicators before and after the restart: did it do what its diagnosis expected?
5. **The prompt** (`system_v3.md`): the physics primer, how to read the description, the maps and the indicators; to explain a blocked run physically before acting, to prefer a targeted restart when the design is at fault, and to state in `expected_effect` what the restart should change physically (the hot spot gone, the load path closed, less gray material).

In, in `gemseo-lso`:

- the live run gives the multipliers and the stationarity of the last iteration per variable on request (the price and KKT maps);
- the topology model and its disciplines implement `PhysicalDesign`: the description of the bracket and of the cantilever (their features: the reentrant corner of the bracket), the physical density, the von Mises stress, the strain energy density, the sign of the major principal stress, the displacement;
- `run_piloted_topology.py` prints the maps of the result and its indicators.

Journal and report: the maps and indicators sent are recorded in the journal (`design` records); the post-run report describes the final design physically, with its density map, and each restart with the maps before and after.

Out:

- **the data levels** (§ 7.1) for the physical description and the maps: decided later (settled after the plan: sent at every level, see the notes);
- maps as images (PNG), and layouts that are not 2D grids (unstructured meshes, 3D): plan 72;
- transformations declared by the model itself;
- the Copilot view of the application showing the maps (the journal already holds them): plan 72;
- the oscillation of the KKT residual at the end of the runs of the bracket and the stop criteria: plan 73.

## Implementation notes

- **Package** `gemseo_claude_pilot.design`, one module per subject: `description` (the protocol `PhysicalDesign`, the checked `PhysicalDescription`, the `Grid` and its reduction by blocks), `view` (the points and what Claude reads), `maps`, `indicators`, `transforms`, `source` (the discipline found on the scenario, the points prepared, the restart). Specified in CLAUDE_PILOT_SPEC § 4.8.
- **The model's fields are computed in the optimizer's thread**, at each check of the pilot (the model is not computing then; the advisor thread never calls it): at the current point, which the model has at hand, and at the best point, computed again only when it changes (one finite-element solve, and one more for the optimizer's next rows). A past evaluation has the design variable and the constraints only (the tools say so). A model whose fields fail is used without them, with a warning.
- **Sizes of the constraints**: GEMSEO knows the size of a vector constraint only once it is evaluated; the pilot's first snapshot says 1. The description is checked against a constraint's size only when it is known, and a constraint whose values do not fit its cells at run time is not laid on the grid.
- **Thresholds** of the indicators: solid above a density of 0.5 (load path, members, 4-connected), gray between 0.1 and 0.9, active stress above a ratio of 0.98, dead material below 1 % of the mean strain energy of the solid, a hot spot at a feature within three minimum member sizes of it; five zones at most per indicator. Positions and the transformations are in map cells (row 0 at the bottom): Claude reads and acts on the same coordinates.
- **Points**: `best`, `current`, and `start` before any evaluation (the review sees the starting design).
- **Restart**: the other design variables are those of the base point; the design variable is transformed normalized by its bounds, before the model's filter, and clipped into them. The pilot counts the restarts applied (`restarts` in the pilot section of the context, and for the guardrails). A restart is not offered (`allowed_actions`) without a description.
- **Measured**: a context of the bracket at 10⁴ elements of 2,800 tokens with the detail of the design (maps and indicators), 1,900 without it, 1,160 without the design; the design view prepared in about a millisecond at 64 elements.
- **The large-scale optimizer**: its live run gives `multipliers()` (the multipliers of each inequality constraint, GEMSEO's names, times the scale of the objective: in the units of the problem) and `stationarity()` (the projected gradient of the Lagrangian per design variable, in the space of the optimizer); the adapter gives the slices of the constraints, the optimizer keeps its last projected gradient (`stationarity`).
- **The topology benchmark** describes its physics: the grid of its mask, the supports and loads on the elements touching their nodes, the reentrant corner of the bracket, the physical density, the relaxed stress ratio, the strain energy, the sign of the principal stress of largest magnitude and the displacement of each element; `run_piloted_topology.py` prints the density map and the indicators of its result.
- **Dependency**: SciPy is now a direct dependency of the copilot (`scipy.ndimage`, `cKDTree`), BSD-3-Clause, already in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md with GEMSEO.
- **Data levels**: at the user's request, the design is sent whatever the data level, `anonymized` included, with its real names and values: Claude needs the physics to judge the design.
- **Tests**: maps character by character, the reduction of 125 × 125, each indicator on designs drawn as text, each transformation, `connect` closing a cut load path, the checks of a description, the guardrails of a restart, the detector `stuck`, a stuck run's context with its maps and the `design` journal record, the tools, the design at every data level, the budget at 10⁴ elements, and a piloted bracket restarted from a rounded corner (the new segment starts from the transformed design, the next contexts compare the design, the report ends with its maps); the live multipliers and stationarity; the description and fields of the bracket.
- **After the first piloted run** (the bracket at 10⁴ elements, stopped by the user at 80 outer iterations and 173 evaluations of 600, a volume of 0.3403 slightly infeasible): Claude lowered `move_limit` live, and proposed the right restart (a void disk of four elements at the reentrant corner, where the prices were highest), which expired before being applied. Fixed:
  - Claude Sonnet 5 took more than the 3 minutes of a call twice, the call carrying the maps included: both roles now use Claude Opus 5.5 at a low effort by default (a preference, `GEMSEO_CLAUDE_PILOT_EFFORT`), the models chosen in lists;
  - a decision on an LSO algorithm expired after 50 evaluations, 77 of which went by in 26 GCMMA iterations: it now expires after 10 outer iterations;
  - `stuck` fired at iteration 21 while the objective fell fast, the iterates slightly infeasible: infeasibility counts only once the violation no longer halves;
  - Claude read tiny steps in the history (GCMMA's inner evaluations) and a stalled run: the context and the prompt (version 4) say to read the optimizer's iterations;
  - the note of the reentrant corner asserted that it keeps the constraints violated: it now asks to check the hot spots and prices;
  - the user found the run spent too many iterations for little gain: a call every 10 outer iterations, or at the first one 10 s after the end of the last answer (`answer_pause`), the triggers checked at each outer iteration; the piloted bracket allows 300 calls; and a `plateau` event (less than 0.5 % over 10 outer iterations) asking Claude to stop or restart the run.
- **Steering** (at the user's request, after the first piloted run: the optimum in as few iterations as possible, Claude never blocking the optimizer): a `steer` decision moves the current design — `anticipate` (the trend extrapolated), transformations on the grid, values of variables — applied live by the large-scale optimizer (`LiveRun.move`, `Optimizer.move`: one evaluation, its state kept) or as a new segment for other algorithms, computed at application from the last iterate; the context shows at every call where the design heads (`design.heading`: the trend map over 5 iterates and the anticipated design with its indicators: 3,700 tokens at 10⁴ elements); the first call comes after the first outer iteration (`first_iteration`), to judge the starting point; the application offers `restart` and `steer` among the actions of a driver's copilot.
- **Second and third piloted runs** (the bracket at 10⁴ elements, launched from the session): in the second, Claude restarted at iteration 3 to round the corner, tuned the screening live, then stopped the run at 117 evaluations, judging it in a long tail while its iterates, 1 % above the limit, still fell (its best feasible point 0.490). The prompt (version 5) now asks to judge the progress on the iterates, to know that the optimizer restores feasibility at the end, to stop only once the objective no longer falls, and not to reduce `move_limit` below a violation of 1 %; the design anticipated from the trend is smoothed over half the minimum member size (extrapolated raw, it showed floating parts and checkerboards Claude could not trust). In the third, Claude steered the corner round at iteration 1, let the run converge (iterates at 0.3464, the level of the plain run), and found that the restoration never started: the core counted `max_iter` in iterations, GEMSEO in evaluations (fixed: `max_evaluations`). Its blend with an early design to regain feasibility raised the violation from 0.2 % to 22 % in the last iteration: the prompt now leaves the end to the optimizer. 94 calls, 12 s each (median).
