# 56 — Bi-level with 100,000 variables on sensitivities

Status: Done
Depends on: 55
SPEC: § 15.3

## Goal

A variant of the bi-level wing sizing with 100,000 variables, converging quickly thanks to gradients, sensitivities and active sets (the user's request).

## Scope

In `examples/wingBiLevel100k/`:
- `twist_optimizer/`: TwistOptimizer, the 50,000 twists of the sections minimizing the drag (strip theory, profile and induced drag), with L-BFGS-B;
- `wing_box_optimizer/`: WingBoxOptimizer, the 50,000 skin thicknesses minimizing the weight under a tip deflection limit (unit load method), with MMA;
- `flight.py`: Loads and Performance;
- `wing_bilevel_100k.py`: SystemOptimizer (area, span, design weight), SLSQP;
- `sensitivities.py`: at the optimum, the active set of each level with its multipliers, and the post-optimal derivatives of the sub-optima.

Out:
- Changes to the application: it reads, generates and runs the study as it is.
- The normal/surrogate duo of wingBiLevel: a surrogate cannot learn a function of 50,000 inputs.

## Implementation notes

What 100,000 variables require of GEMSEO 6.3, found while prototyping:

- **`log_problem=False`** on the sub-optimizations: GEMSEO logs the design space as a table with a row per component at the start and end of each optimization; with 50,000 components, the logging took minutes.
- **Not the BiLevel formulation**: the post-optimal analysis only differentiates the objective of a sub-optimization, and BiLevel gives the system its local variables and couplings too. The sub-optimizations are adapters built by hand, whose only output is their optimum (drag, weight), in an MDF system level: every system function then depends on optima only, and SLSQP gets exact gradients.
- **No loop at system level**: the weight ↔ load loop reran the wing box optimization about 8 times per system evaluation. The system chooses a design weight instead, the wing box is sized for it, and a constraint makes the weights agree (active at the optimum). Each evaluation runs each sub-optimization once: 7 times faster.
- **Bounds never active**: GEMSEO's Lagrange multipliers build the active bounds as a dense matrix (active × 50,000): with thousands of active bounds, gigabytes and a least-squares problem that never ends. The optima are kept inside the bounds by design.
- **Relative thicknesses**: the thickness a wing needs varies as span⁵/area³, over orders of magnitude on the system design space; from one system point to the next, MMA had to move thousands of thicknesses to their bound. Relative to the uniform thickness meeting the limit under a reference load, the thicknesses stay between 0.2 and 2; MMA converges in a few iterations, and the multiplier of the deflection still carries the derivative with respect to the load.
- **The system design space** keeps the structure feasible: at its corners, the thicknesses stay far from their bounds.
- **MMA** reaches the optimum within 1e-5 in about 25 iterations, then dithers around the constraint without meeting a tighter criterion: 30 iterations at most. SLSQP stops at a relative change of 1e-6, the noise of the sub-optimizations.
- **KS aggregation of 50,000 stresses** was tried first: MMA oscillated (a KS with a large ρ is nearly a max), and the augmented Lagrangian of GEMSEO converged but took minutes. The tip deflection, a sum of terms in 1/thickness, is separable: exactly what MMA approximates.
- **A relative weight margin** (`weight/design_weight - 1`): in newtons, the margin at the optimum (−0.03 N) was not within the tolerance of the active set.
- No test runs the demo at full scale (26 s); the reading test replaces 50,000 sections by 100 in a copy.
