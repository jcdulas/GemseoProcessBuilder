## Goal

The optimizer is measured on the problem it is made for: stress-constrained topology optimization, with a density and a von Mises constraint per element, from 10⁴ to 10⁵ elements. The measures settle the defaults, the stale rows (open point 2), the scaling of the constraints (open point 3) and the dual solver (open point 6).

## Scope

In:
- `benchmarks/topology/`: a 2D plane-stress finite-element model in NumPy and `scipy.sparse` (bilinear quadrilaterals on a regular grid, SIMP interpolation, a density filter), written as GEMSEO disciplines: compliance or volume as objective, one relaxed von Mises constraint per element (the ε-relaxation or qp-approach, needed by the stress singularity);
- rows by adjoint with the factorization of the stiffness matrix reused: the model implements `RowJacobian` and computes the rows of a batch with one multi-right-hand-side solve; and directional derivatives by the tangent (direct) mode with the same factorization, with the pattern of each stress constraint within a radius of its element, for the colored and hybrid modes (the stresses are coupled to the whole structure: approximate sparsity);
- the classic cases: L-shaped bracket and cantilever beam, from 10⁴ to 10⁵ elements;
- runs of `LSO_MMA` and `LSO_GCMMA` with screening, `always` and `near_active`, and with the colored and hybrid Jacobians (evaluations per iteration, share of each row outside the pattern by `probe_sparsity`, as a function of the radius); the reference run without screening at 10⁴ elements; `NLOPT_MMA` on an aggregated (p-norm) version at 10⁴ elements for comparison;
- recorded for each run: iterations, rows computed and reused, repairs, optimizer and model times, peak memory, final objective and maximum stress; final designs saved as images;
- a report in `benchmarks/results/` and the tuned defaults.

Out:
- 3D models; other physics.

## Tests

- The finite-element model on a tiny grid: displacements of a known case; rows against finite differences; the relaxed stress constraint at full density equals von Mises.
- A 10 × 5 bracket optimized in under one second: a feasible design (the full runs are benchmarks, not tests).

## Acceptance criteria

- At 10⁴ elements, the screened run reaches the objective of the run without screening within 1 %, with at most 1/10 of its rows.
- At 10⁵ elements, a feasible optimized bracket; the time per iteration and the peak memory recorded.
- `python tools/check.py` passes.
