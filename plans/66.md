# 66 — Large-scale optimizer: screening and row cache

Status: Done
Depends on: 65
SPEC: [LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md](../docs/LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md) § 3.2–3.5, 4, 7

## Goal

The optimizer asks only for the rows it needs: the working set of the constraints close to activity, checked at every new point with all the constraint values, and a row cache that may reuse stale rows. On large synthetic problems, it finds the same optimum as without screening with 10 to 20 times fewer rows.

## Scope

In:
- `core/screening.py`: the working set (margin δ_k and its decrease, last active constraints, hysteresis, cap by the largest values), § 3.3;
- the screening check after each candidate step: screened-out constraints violated or too close join the working set, their rows are computed at x_k and the subproblem is solved again, at most `max_screening_repairs` times (§ 3.2, step 5);
- `core/rows.py`: the row cache by constraint index, with the iterate of each row; the policies `always` and `near_active` (age, step), § 3.5; dense and sparse rows;
- the batch request of rows to the problem (`constraint_rows(x, rows)`), split by `row_batch_size`;
- a few equality constraints as pairs of inequalities with a shrinking tolerance (§ 3.4);
- the report gets the screening figures: |S|, rows computed and reused, repairs;
- `benchmarks/synthetic.py`: a separable quadratic objective with local constraints and a known solution, of 10⁴ to 10⁶ variables and constraints, with a counter of rows; a script that runs it and writes its results (rows, iterations, optimizer time, peak memory) to `benchmarks/results/`.

Out:
- GEMSEO (plan 68); topology optimization (plan 69).

## Tests

- A problem whose active set is known: the same optimum with screening and with `max_working_set` = m; fewer rows with screening.
- A screened-out constraint that the step would violate: repaired, the accepted iterate satisfies it.
- The cap `max_working_set`: the largest values kept.
- `near_active`: a stale row is reused within its age and step, refreshed beyond; a run with stale rows still reaches the optimum of the synthetic problem (small size).
- Sparse rows give the same iterates as the same rows dense.
- Equality pairs: HS problems with equality constraints (HS 6, 7).
- Every test under one second (the benchmark runs are not tests).

## Acceptance criteria

- On the synthetic problem of 10⁵ variables and constraints, the optimum is found with at most 1/10 of the rows of the full Jacobian per iteration, and the optimizer time per iteration meets § 3.7.
- `python tools/check.py` passes.

## Implementation notes

- **Screening margin**: twice the largest change of a constraint over the last step, between `screening_margin_min` (0.05) and `screening_margin` (0.3), instead of a fixed schedule. The hysteresis keeps a constraint of the last working set within `keep_factor` (1.5) times that margin: a fixed `keep_margin` of 0.5, wider than the margin itself, kept 17.6 % of the rows per iteration on the synthetic problem; relative to the margin, 8.1 %.
- **Repairs** only for a screened-out constraint that the step would **violate**; one that only comes closer enters the next working set by itself. A repair may exceed `max_working_set`; after `max_screening_repairs`, the step is accepted (the spec said it would be shortened).
- **Equality constraints**: pairs `±h - ε <= 0` whose band ε starts at `max(1, max |h(x0)|)` and halves at each iteration (`equality_band_decrease`) down to `eq_tolerance`. With ε at `eq_tolerance` from the start, MMA crawled along curved constraints (HS 6, HS 7 not converged in 300 iterations); with the band, GCMMA solves HS 7 in 25 iterations. HS 6 (a parabola) stays hard.
- **Rows in float32** (`row_dtype`, the default, at the user's request): half the memory and the time of the products; the products are made in the precision of the rows (casting the vector, not the rows) and returned in float64. The first version computed the approximations through `r`, subtracting sums of the size of the gradients: in float32 the KKT residual stalled at 1e-5 and the optimum was off by 1e-5. The approximations are now evaluated around the iterate (every term proportional to the step), and float32 rows meet a KKT tolerance of 1e-6 like float64 rows (test).
- **KKT tolerance 1e-3 by default** (at the user's request): 20 to 45 % fewer iterations on the test problems, the variables within about 1e-3 of the optimum and the objective within 1e-7; the tests of accuracy ask for 1e-6.
- **MMA cycles on the synthetic problem** (a quadratic objective): near the optimum, a variable with no active constraint has a gradient going to zero and MMA's approximation of it becomes too flat; the asymptotes shrink to their floor and the iterates alternate between two points. GCMMA converges. Documented in the spec (§ 3.6) and the README: MMA for structural problems, GCMMA for the others. The synthetic benchmark uses GCMMA.
- **The row cache is not saved** in the state (it may hold gigabytes): with `always`, a resumed run is still identical to an uninterrupted one; with `near_active`, its first iteration computes its rows again. States can be saved to a binary file object: the test saves in memory, since writing an HDF5 file on this machine took up to 11 s (first import of h5py, and the antivirus scanning the new file); `tests/conftest.py` imports h5py, nlopt and SciPy at collection time.
- **Sparse rows** are used as such everywhere (stacking, products, KKT); only the interior point, for small subproblems, makes them dense.
- **Synthetic problem**: `gemseo_lso.benchmarks.synthetic.LocalConstraints` — one local constraint (three neighbouring variables) per variable, a chosen share active at a known solution; `benchmarks/run_synthetic.py` measures it.
- **Measured at 10⁵ variables and constraints** (GCMMA, 2 % active, 12 cores): the working set asks for 8,460 rows per iteration (8.1 % of the full Jacobian) and reaches the known solution to 3.3e-4 (the KKT tolerance of 1e-3) in 22 iterations, the optimizer taking 2.1 s per iteration; with young rows reused, 7,946 rows (7.6 %). The run at 10⁶ was stopped: the benchmark takes too long (more than 10 minutes, the reference asking for every row); its speed is left to a later optimization, the reference is now limited to 10⁵. The results file of the earlier runs is not committed.
- **Profile at 10⁵ (after the plan)**: 6 iterations took 36 s, 87 % in the dual subproblems and 13 % in the row cache. The cache split every block into single rows, one Python call each (4.7 s for 90,000 sparse rows), and `near_active` computed the move of the iterate once per row (72,000 times over 10⁵ values): it now keeps the blocks with, for each constraint, its block and position, gathers them with one index per block, and computes the move once per iteration — 0.16 to 0.19 s. A looser tolerance of the dual (1e-7 or 1e-8 instead of 1e-10) changed nothing: L-BFGS-B never reaches it. It stops on no progress, or on a failed line search, with a projected gradient of 1e-2 to 1e-5 in float32 (the rounding of the products), and needs even more evaluations in float64 (9,905 against 4,247, down to 1e-7): the dual is badly conditioned, above all in the first two subproblems (1,867 and 706 evaluations), from a start violating many constraints. The remedy is a Newton method on the dual, whose Hessian is sparse when the rows are: left to a later plan.
- **Tolerances aligned** (at the user's request): `kkt_tolerance` 1e-3, `dual_tolerance` 1e-5 (a setting now; it was 1e-10), and `ineq_tolerance`, `eq_tolerance` raised from 1e-6 to 1e-5 — the dual leaves a violation of the approximated constraints up to its tolerance, and a feasibility asked tighter would never be declared. The tests run on these defaults; their checks follow (the variables within 1e-3, 2e-2 on the cantilever whose objective gradient is 0.06).
- **The optimizer adjusts itself** (at the user's request: it must not fail and leave the tolerances to be played with):
  - the tolerance of the dual is a tenth of the last step, at most 1e-2, at least `dual_tolerance` and a tenth of the feasibility tolerances. Tied first to the KKT residual, it made a circle on HS 21 (a coarse dual, inaccurate multipliers, a residual kept high); at 10⁵, the full run went from 35 s (20 iterations) to 24 s (23 iterations, 1.05 s each), the solution from 1.8e-3 to 1.6e-4;
  - the band of the equality pairs stops at half `eq_tolerance`, leaving room for what the dual leaves (HS 7 was stuck at |h| = 1.3e-5 for 1e-5);
  - MMA switches to GCMMA when its KKT residual has not decreased by 10 % over `kkt_stall_iterations` (10) while its iterates still move: HS 21 converges in 20 iterations (switch at 10), the synthetic problem in 47 (switch at 39), where MMA cycled to `max_iter`;
  - at feasible points, no progress while the iterates have settled (moves under 1e-3) ends the run as `stalled`, with the residual reached;
  - the KKT residual is relative to the largest component of the gradient (at least a thousandth of it at the start), not to `max(1, ‖∇f‖)`: the cantilever no longer needs a looser check.

- **Speed at 10⁶ variables and constraints** (after the plan, at the user's request): the L-BFGS-B dual stalled in the first subproblem. A projected Newton method on the dual (`dual_solver="newton"`, chosen by `auto` for sparse rows; spec § 3.7) and fused kernels over the variables, parallel with Numba (`core/kernels.py`, `core/_numba_kernels.py`; a new dependency, BSD) bring the first iteration from 127 s to 12 s and the run at 10⁵ from 24 s to 3 s. The kernels are compiled once and cached on disk; the tests compile them before running (`conftest.py`, `warm_up`), and check that the NumPy and Numba versions compute the same numbers. The results differ only by the order of the sums, which a coarse dual turns into iterates a few 1e-3 apart: the synthetic check of the MMA → GCMMA switch now accepts 5e-3, like the other known optima.
- **Vectorized kernels** (at the user's request): `error_model="numpy"` and `fastmath` limited to reordering the sums let LLVM vectorize the loops, and `primal_numba` recomputes (u − x_k)² and (x_k − l)² instead of reading them: 3.65 ms to 2.88 ms an evaluation at 10⁶ variables, `curvature_numba` 5.28 ms to 4.70 ms. The loops are bound by memory reads: SIMD alone gained 3 %. Over a run the gain is 5 to 8 % an iteration, below the noise of this machine (Defender scans).
- **The cap kept below the active set** (found at 10⁶): with 2 % of 10⁶ constraints active, 20,057 for a `max_working_set` of 20,000, the cap dropped 57 active constraints with their multipliers. The subproblem ignored them, a repair added one back at every iteration, and the KKT residual, computed on the working set, missed their terms: it stayed at 0.5 while the multipliers were exact to 1e-5, and the run ended `stalled` after 43 iterations (150 to 200 s). The constraints active in the last subproblem are now kept whatever the cap, which only limits the others: the run converges in 25 iterations and 43 s, without repairs after the start, 0.3 to 0.7 s an iteration.
