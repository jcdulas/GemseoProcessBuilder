# 67 — Large-scale optimizer: colored sparse Jacobians

Status: Done
Depends on: 66
SPEC: [LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md](../docs/LARGE_SCALE_OPTIMIZER_SPEC.md) § 3.9, 5

## Goal

On large surfaces (hulls, car bodies, aircraft), a constraint depends on a few neighbouring variables: its row of the Jacobian is sparse. The optimizer then obtains **the whole Jacobian** from a few tens of directional derivatives (tangent mode of the model) instead of one gradient per constraint: variables that never share a constraint are perturbed together — a coloring of the columns (Curtis, Powell and Reid, 1974) — and one derivative in the direction of a color gives the entries of all its variables. The number of colors depends on the size of a neighbourhood, not on the size of the problem.

Mechanical constraints (buckling of a panel computed over 2 m of an 80 m hull) are mostly local but coupled to the whole structure through its state: their Jacobian is sparse only approximately, and the far couplings leak into the colored entries. The colors therefore **overlap**, at the price of more directional derivatives:

- a **margin** around the pattern (`pattern_margin`): the coloring is made on a widened neighbourhood, so that the variables of a color are farther apart than the nominal zone of influence, and leak less into each other;
- **several colorings** (`color_overlap`, 2 by default), each variable in one color of each, with other companions each time: every entry is measured several times in different mixtures, recovered by least squares over the pattern, and **the spread of its measures estimates its error**.

The **hybrid** mode then adds exact rows for the constraints close to activity — the few that decide the step — and for those whose colored entries disagree beyond `leakage_tolerance`.

## Scope

In:
- the protocol (§ 5) gets two optional methods: `sparsity()`, the pattern of the inequality constraints — a `scipy.sparse` boolean matrix of shape (constraints, variables), `True` where an entry must be considered, given by the user's code (plan 68: a step of the process); the optimizer needs no geometry — and `directional_derivatives(x, directions)`, the products of the Jacobian with a batch of directions (tangent mode; the model may compute them in parallel); without the latter, forward finite differences on the values, with a step `difference_step`;
- the pattern is checked (shape, sparse, no empty row) and widened by `pattern_margin` on the pattern itself: one widening adds to a constraint the variables of the constraints sharing a variable with it (the neighbours' neighbours), with no coordinates;
- `core/coloring.py`: a greedy coloring of the columns (largest degree first) so that no two columns of a color share a row of the pattern widened by `pattern_margin` (the pattern of the neighbours' neighbours, `pattern_margin` times); `color_overlap` different colorings (other orders of the columns); the check that a coloring is valid;
- the recovery: each entry of the pattern from the products of the colors of its variable, by least squares over the colorings; the spread of its measures as its estimated error (the leakage);
- `core/sparse_jacobian.py`: the colored Jacobian at an iterate (one batch of `colors` directions), kept as a sparse matrix; optionally updated without any evaluation between two colorings by Schubert's sparse quasi-Newton update (from the change of the constraint values over the step), and computed again every `jacobian_refresh` iterations;
- the setting `jacobian_mode`, **the colored modes optional and off by default**: `rows` (the default: plans 65 and 66), `colored` (every row from the colored Jacobian), `hybrid` (the colored Jacobian, and exact rows, from the row cache, for the constraints within `fresh_margin` of activity and for those whose estimated leakage exceeds `leakage_tolerance` relatively to their row);
- **without a pattern, the matrix is taken as full**: the optimizer runs as in plans 65 and 66 (working set and row cache), with no sparse optimization — coloring a full pattern would take one color per variable, one directional derivative per variable, worse than the rows; `colored` or `hybrid` asked for without a pattern is refused with a message; a pattern given while `jacobian_mode` is `rows` is not used;
- the report and the result count directional derivatives and exact rows apart;
- `probe_sparsity(problem, x, rows)`: computes a few exact rows and the colored Jacobian at the same point, and reports the share of each row inside the pattern and the error of the colored entries — to choose between `colored` and `hybrid`;
- the synthetic problem (plan 66) gets a stencil pattern and an optional far coupling (a small global term in every constraint, decaying with distance), to test both cases.

Out:
- the directional derivatives through GEMSEO (plan 68);
- colorings for the equality constraints (few, § 3.4: their rows are asked for as before).

## Tests

- Coloring: no two columns of a color share a row of the widened pattern; a 5-point 2D stencil needs at most 13 colors without margin, whatever the grid size, and more with it; `color_overlap` colorings put each variable with other companions.
- Overlap: with far coupling, the least-squares entries are closer to the true ones than those of a single coloring, and the estimated leakage follows the true error (same order, correlated over the entries); without far coupling, the leakage is zero.
- Recovery: on an exactly sparse problem, the colored Jacobian equals the true one to rounding (directional derivatives) and to the step (finite differences).
- `colored` on the synthetic problem without far coupling: the known optimum, with `colors` directional derivatives per iteration.
- `hybrid` with far coupling: the known optimum, `colored` alone off by more; exact rows only for the constraints near activity or of large leakage.
- Schubert update: exact for linear constraints after one step; the run reaches the optimum with fewer directional derivatives.
- `probe_sparsity`: the share outside the pattern is 0 without far coupling, and measured with it.
- By default (`rows`), a problem giving a pattern runs as before, with the same iterates; without a pattern, `colored` and `hybrid` are refused.
- Every test under one second.

## Acceptance criteria

- On the synthetic problem of 10⁵ variables and constraints with a 2D stencil, the Jacobian of each iteration costs at most 30 directional derivatives for the first coloring (and one widening more for each further one: see the notes), and the optimum is found.
- With far coupling, `hybrid` finds the optimum with exact rows for at most the constraints within `fresh_margin` of activity.
- `python tools/check.py` passes.

## Implementation notes

- **Optional, off by default** (at the user's request): `jacobian_mode` is `rows` by default and has no `auto`; `colored` and `hybrid` are asked for explicitly, and refused without a pattern. A problem giving a pattern runs in the rows mode as before, with the same iterates. In GEMSEO Process Builder, a "Colored Jacobians" checkbox of the driver, unchecked by default (plan 68, spec § 6).
- **Protocol**: `sparsity()` and `directional_derivatives(x, directions)` may be absent or return `None`; the directions are a sparse `(n, k)` matrix. `DenseProblem` takes an optional `pattern` (colored Jacobians by finite differences). The synthetic problem gives its pattern and a tangent mode, and takes `grid` (a 2D stencil), `coupling` (a far coupling over 8 variables on each side, decaying with distance, outside the pattern) and `tangent`.
- **Recovery**: every coloring is valid on the pattern, so each reading of an entry is the entry plus what leaks into it; the least-squares estimate over the colorings is the mean of the readings, and their spread (standard deviation) the estimated error. The leakage of a row is the norm of its spreads relative to the norm of the row.
- **Each coloring on a pattern widened once more** (a deviation from the plan, which had the colorings differ only by their order): colorings of the same widened pattern come out nearly periodic on a regular stencil and leak the same far entries, so their spread missed the error — correlated negatively with it over the rows (Spearman −0.66 on the ring). With margins 1 and 2, the estimate follows the error (+1.0 on the ring, +0.67 on a 2D grid, medians within 5 %) and the entries are 30 % more accurate, at the price of more colors: on the 2D grid, 25 and 53 at 10⁵ instead of 25 and 23. The acceptance criterion now bounds the first coloring.
- **The greedy coloring** is a Numba loop (`kernels.greedy`, with the same loop in Python without Numba, same colors): two colorings of 10⁶ variables take 5 s on a 2D grid, once per run (again only when `pattern_margin` or `color_overlap` change).
- **Hybrid**: the exact rows are those of the working set within `fresh_margin` of activity or leaking beyond `leakage_tolerance`, taken through the row cache and merged with the colored ones in order. Near the optimum the working set shrinks to the active constraints, all exact: the saving is on the inactive ones, and the exact rows per iteration are about the active set.
- **`colored` alone with a far coupling** converges to the optimum of the colored Jacobian and declares it, the KKT residual being measured with the same rows (x off by 5e-2 on the synthetic problem, for 1 % of leakage): documented (spec § 3.9); `hybrid` measures it with exact rows. A later safeguard could check the active rows once at convergence.
- **Schubert's update** runs only when `jacobian_refresh` > 1, after each accepted step, on the step and the change of all the constraint values; with 3, a third of the directional derivatives, the same iterations.
- **Not saved**: the colored Jacobian, like the row cache; a resumed run colors again at its first iteration (the same iterates with `jacobian_refresh` 1; test). `STATE_VERSION` 4: the count of directional derivatives is saved.
- **Measured** (`benchmarks/run_synthetic.py`, GCMMA, 2 % active, 12 cores):
