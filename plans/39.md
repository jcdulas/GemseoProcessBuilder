## Implementation notes

- **Origins** (`origin`):
  - an approximation linearization mode (`set_jacobian_approximation`) gives `approximated`;
  - a nested driver (`MDOScenarioAdapter`) is `approximated`: the derivatives of an optimum are sensitivities;
  - a group is as good as its worst member;
  - otherwise `exact` when the class overrides `_compute_jacobian`, else `missing`.
  - GEMSEO approximates the Jacobian of an `AutoPyDiscipline` without `py_jac` by itself: it is `approximated`, not `missing`.
  - Nodes are found by the discipline names of the script mapping (`mapping["disciplines"]`); the app merges the origins of every dry-run target, in the background, 1.5 s after the resolution changes.
- **Checks** (`check`):
  - Driver: the objective and constraints (`problem.objective`, `problem.constraints`) with respect to `design_space.variable_names`, on the top-level process of the formulation:
    - one top-level discipline as is;
    - MDF: an `MDAChain` of its disciplines;
    - IDF: an `MDOParallelChain`, since the couplings are design variables and inputs of every discipline;
    - otherwise an `MDOChain`.
    - The point is the current design value, or the middle of the bounds for a DOE.
  - Component: the process runs once first, so that the coupled inputs are computed rather than left at their zero defaults.
  - Comparison: `linearize` against `DisciplineJacApprox` with centered differences.
    - The step is `1e-6 × max(|x|, 0.01)` per component: an absolute step gave false errors for small values (an inertia of 1e-4).
    - The error is `max|J − A| / max(1, max|A|)`, tolerance 1e-4.
    - Integer inputs are cast to floats.
  - A `NotImplementedError` (BiLevel's `MDOWarmStartedChain`) becomes a clear message.
- Verified on the examples: Sellar MDF, IDF (25 pairs) and DisciplinaryOpt, wing sizing, beam chain (DOE), Rosenbrock, the external code (approximated) and a component of the sequence. Errors are between 1e-11 and 4e-8.
  - Through the nested optimizer of the sequence, the derivatives differ by up to 60 %: expected for sensitivities of an optimum, and noted in the result.
- **Gradients view**: `reader.gradients` reads `history.h5` with GEMSEO's `Database` (the `@<function>` entries) for the objective and constraints. The view draws the norms on a log scale and a table of the last gradients, with bars showing their size.
- Line charts show only whole ticks on integer axes (a short history repeated "1, 2, 2, 3").
