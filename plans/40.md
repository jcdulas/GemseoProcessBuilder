# 40 — Response surfaces and large results

Status: Done
Depends on: 39
SPEC: § 12.1, § 12.2

## Goal

The user asked for a response map: "a 2D or 3D projection of the design space with metamodels (Kriging, neural networks) to see the shape of the optimization landscape and the boundary of the constraints". Runs will have hundreds of thousands of variables and many responses: every chart must be able to filter them by active set, sensitivities and gradients.

## Scope

In:
- runner: `dataset.npy` beside `dataset.csv`;
- reader:
  - binary values;
  - columns by name in constant time;
  - the best evaluation;
  - rows of chosen columns;
  - gradients of chosen variables and functions;
- `results/ranking.py` (sensitivity, gradient, active set) and the worker methods `results.ranking`, `results.response_surface`, `results.surface_algorithms`;
- page:
  - `lib/results_filter.js` and `lib/surface_3d.js` (pure, tested);
  - the filter bar;
  - the views following it;
  - the Response surface view.

Out:
- Sobol indices or other global sensitivity analyses (they need their own sampling).
- Pinning the other design variables of a response surface at chosen values (they stay at the best evaluation).

## Acceptance criteria

- A Sellar DOE of 120 evaluations: Kriging predicts `c_1` and `c_2` with R² = 1.00 on the evaluations left aside; the ranking of `c_1` puts `x_shared[0]` first.
- A synthetic run of 300 evaluations × 100,000 design variables and 200 constraints stays usable (see the notes).
- `python tools/check.py` passes.

## Implementation notes

- **Reading large runs**: parsing the text of 300 × 100,000 values took 12.6 s (pandas' parser is slower on wide tables, 17 s). The runner now also saves `dataset.npy` (column-major), loaded in 0.23 s; the reader falls back to the CSV for older runs. `RunTable.values` is column-major too: the views read columns.
- **Measured on 300 evaluations × 100,000 variables**:

  | Step | Time |
  | --- | --- |
  | Load | 0.29 s |
  | Columns | 0.01 s (8.6 MB of JSON) |
  | History of 400 columns | 0.01 s |
  | Ranking by sensitivity | 0.9–1.2 s |
  | Active set | 0.06 s |
  | Kriging surface of 2 responses on 8 variables | 5.1 s |
  | Neural network surface on 20 variables | 0.24 s |

- **The page reads columns on demand**: beyond 400 columns, `ResultsSource` reads the responses and the first design variables with the run, the others when a view shows them (`ensure`). The table asks the worker only for its columns (`names`).
- **Sensitivity**:
  - The score is the larger of the absolute correlations of the response with the variable and with its squared distance to its mean.
  - With the linear correlation alone, Sellar's `c_1` (through `x_shared[0]²` over [-10, 10]) ranked `x_shared[0]` last.
  - It uses at most 5,000 evenly spaced evaluations, and columns by blocks of 4,096.
- **Gradient**: the gradient at the best evaluation, or at the last one with a gradient (the note says so), times the width of the bounds (of the values without bounds).
- **Active set**: a variable within 1e-6 of its range from a bound is at it. An inequality within `max(1e-4, 1e-3 × max|g|)` of zero is active; above 1e-4 it is violated (GEMSEO's tolerance). An equality is active unless violated.
- **Response surface**:
  - One GEMSEO regression model per response. With one shared model, Kriging's length scales were spoiled by the outliers of Sellar's objective (`exp(-y2)` up to 3,748), and `c_1` got R² ≈ 0.
  - Each input column is a scalar variable of the dataset, so a single element of a large vector can be learned.
  - Kriging takes 2 optimizer restarts instead of GEMSEO's 10: four times faster, with the same quality on the tests. Without restarts, the fit often failed.
  - R² is measured on a random fifth of the evaluations, then the model learns from all of them.
  - When the filter keeps all the design variables, the view ranks them by sensitivity to the chosen response: the axes and the metamodel take the most sensitive ones.
- **Drawing**:
  - 2D uses `d3.contours` for the filled levels and the infeasible regions.
  - The boundaries are isolines computed by `isoSegments` (marching squares): the filled contours of d3 also follow the edges of the grid.
  - 3D sorts the cells of a 40 × 40 grid from the farthest (painter's algorithm), in SVG; the evaluations outside the values of the surface are not drawn.
- **Wing sizing example**: it now uses NLOPT_MMA. SLSQP stopped at its first line search from half of the starting points near the design point, and CI on Linux found -1960 instead of -2590.
- **Check**: `tools/check_js_syntax.mjs` parses every page module (a variable declared twice in one module stopped the whole page from loading; the unit tests import only `lib/`).
